Laredo: A Border City Shaped by Change

Project 02: Synthesis

← Back to Curated Collection

Introduction

Laredo is known today as a border city with a strong cultural connection to Mexico and a big role in international trade. Many people who live here know that, but they may not know how Laredo became the city it is today.

Many of us may not learn much about this history unless we take the time to look into it. Laredo’s history includes Spanish settlement, different governments, military activity, railroads, and trade.

This overview is mainly for college students and younger generations from Laredo who are curious about how the city developed. The purpose is to make its history easier to understand without having to read a bunch of long articles or books.

Instead of just listing dates and events, I want to show how some of the biggest moments in Laredo’s history connect. One thing that keeps coming up in the sources is Laredo’s location on the Rio Grande.

Even though a lot has changed over time, that location has always played a big part in the city’s history. The government, transportation, and economy changed, but Laredo remained important because of its location.

1. How Laredo Began

Laredo was founded in 1755, and Tomás Sánchez played an important role in the early settlement. The Handbook of Texas article “Laredo, TX” gives a general background on the city and explains how it began during the Spanish colonial period.

Another Handbook of Texas source about Tomás Sánchez focuses more on Sánchez himself. It talks about his background, ranching experience, and leadership in the early community.

Using both sources helped me understand that Laredo’s founding was more than just a date. There were people making decisions about where to settle, how the community would survive, and how it would grow.

The location near the Rio Grande was also important from the beginning. That same location would later affect politics, transportation, military activity, and trade.

In other words, the importance of Laredo’s location did not start with modern bridges and trucks. Geography was already shaping the community hundreds of years ago. That connection becomes a pattern throughout the rest of Laredo’s history.

2. Laredo Between Different Governments

Laredo went through several political changes over time. One of the more interesting examples is the Republic of the Rio Grande in 1840.

According to the Handbook of Texas , Laredo was chosen as the official seat of the Republic of the Rio Grande during a rebellion against Mexico’s Centralist government. The republic did not last very long, but it became an important part of Laredo’s history.

The Handbook of Texas article about the Republic of the Rio Grande Museum also shows how this event is remembered today.

One thing I found interesting is that even though Laredo was officially chosen as the seat, some leaders also operated from Guerrero because they had resources there, including a printing press.

Looking at both sources together shows that history is not always as simple as one version of an event. Laredo may have been the official seat, but the government's actual work could take place elsewhere.

This period also shows why Laredo’s history cannot be described as solely American or solely Mexican. The city was connected to political events on both sides of the border.

Even when governments changed, the people living in Laredo remained in the same community and had to adjust to whatever came next. That makes Laredo different from places farther away from the border because national political changes were also local changes for the people living here.

3. The Border as a Place of Conflict

Laredo’s location was good for trade, but it also made the area important during military conflicts.

Fort McIntosh was established after the Mexican-American War. According to Texas Time Travel , the fort was located near the Rio Grande and became important for military operations in the area.

This shows another side of Laredo’s location. The same geography that helped people move goods and travel through the region also made the city important for defense and military control.

Santos Benavides is another important person from this time period. The Handbook of Texas describes him as a businessman, rancher, politician, and Confederate officer from Laredo.

What stood out to me about Benavides is that his story shows how complicated history can be on the border. He was Mexican American, but he also served in the Confederacy. His life involved local politics, business, and military service.

If I only looked at Fort McIntosh, I would mostly see the military side of Laredo. If I only looked at Benavides, I would see the story of one local leader.

Looking at both together gives a better picture of how national conflicts became part of everyday local history. It also shows that the Civil War and other conflicts may not have looked the same in Laredo as they did elsewhere in the country.

4. Railroads, Politics, and a Growing City

By the late 1800s, Laredo was beginning to change because of new transportation.

The Texas Mexican Railway reached Laredo in 1881. According to the Handbook of Texas , the railroad helped connect the city to other areas in Texas and Mexico. It also made it easier to move people and goods.

The railroad is important because it shows how Laredo’s location became even more useful as technology changed. The city was already in an important spot, but the railroad made those connections faster and stronger.

I think this is one of the clearest examples of the pattern across the whole project. The railroad did not suddenly make Laredo important. Instead, it gave the city a new way to use an advantage it already had because of its location.

A second source from this period is El Democrata Fronterizo , a Spanish-language newspaper published in Laredo in 1910.

This source is different because it is not a historian looking back at the past. It gives us a look at the news and information people were actually reading during that time.

The newspaper also shows that the Spanish language and Mexican culture were still major parts of everyday life in Laredo. This matters because economic growth did not erase the city’s culture.

Looking at the railway and newspaper together shows two sides of Laredo’s growth. One source shows changes in transportation and business, while the other shows what community life and culture looked like during the same general period.

5. From Border Settlement to Modern Trade Center

Laredo continued to change during the 1900s and into the present.

The Handbook of Texas explains that Laredo Air Force Base was connected to World War II and later trained American and international pilots. The base eventually closed in 1973.

This shows that Laredo’s location continued to matter for military reasons even many years after Fort McIntosh. The type of military activity changed, but the city still had strategic importance.

Today, Laredo is especially important because of international trade. The Bureau of Transportation Statistics reports on freight moving between the United States and Mexico and shows how important Laredo is for truck transportation.

To me, this is where the whole history starts to connect.

Laredo started as a settlement near the Rio Grande. Later, the same location became important for military activity and railroads. Today, highways, international bridges, and trucks have taken transportation to a much larger level.

The technology changed, but the location stayed important.

This also changed the way I look at modern Laredo. The truck traffic and international trade we see today are not completely separate from the city’s past. They are another version of something Laredo has been doing for generations: connecting different places and allowing people and goods to move between them.

Conclusion

Looking at all these sources together, I started to see Laredo’s history differently.

At first, the topics seemed separate. The founding of Laredo, the Republic of the Rio Grande, Fort McIntosh, the railroad, the Air Force base, and modern trade all occurred at different times.

But when I looked at them together, they kept connecting back to the same thing: Laredo’s location.

The city had to adjust to different governments, wars, transportation systems, and economic changes. At the same time, Laredo kept a strong connection to Mexican and Hispanic culture.

One of the biggest things I learned is that technology changed more than the city's basic role did. Early settlers used the area because of its location. Military forces later saw value in the same area. Railroads made it easier to move people and goods, and today, highways and international bridges continue that role.

That is why I think Laredo’s history is really a border history.

The border is not just a line that marks where Laredo is located today. It has influenced how the city developed for generations.

Laredo grew because it connected different people, cultures, governments, and economies. That connection has changed over time, but it is still part of what makes Laredo important today.

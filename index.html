Introduction

Introduction The history of Laredo, Texas, dates to 1755, when Tomas Sanchez founded a small settlement along the Rio Grande. When Tomas Sanchez founded Laredo, the city went through many difficult changes. Laredo, Texas, has ties to Spain, Mexico, Texas, and the United States. It has been affected by wars, political grabs, railroads, military bases, and international trade. Even under so much adversity, Laredo maintained such a strong identity that connects both sides of the border. Laredo, Texas, USA, and Nuevo Laredo, México. The total collection that I have brought together shows 16 sources that cover different paths in Laredo's history. I wanted to include more than just articles so that the collection would include books, photographs, historical records, biographies, newspaper articles, and government data. The use of multiple documents and data makes it easier to understand, as each document presents a different perspective. The collection is organized into five sections: Founding and Spanish Colonial Laredo, Border and Political Change, Military and Conflict, Growth and Community Life, and Modern Laredo. Anyone can read the collection from the beginning to the end and go directly to the section that interests them most. Fun fact: when going through all the sources, you can see how important Laredo's location on the Rio Grande has been in much of its history.

What These Sources Show Together After looking at these sources together, one pattern keeps recurring: Laredo's location has played a major role at almost every stage of its history. The Rio Grande helped shape where the original settlement was built, why the area mattered politically and militarily, how railroads and trade developed, and why Laredo is still important today. Another thing that stood out is how often Laredo had to adjust to change. The governments around it changed, borders changed, transportation changed, and the economy changed, but the community continued to develop. At the same time, Laredo kept a strong connection to Mexican and Hispanic culture. Overall, I learned that Laredo's history is not about just one country or time period. It is really a border history. The city grew because it connected different places, people, cultures, and economies, and that is still part of what makes Laredo important today.
